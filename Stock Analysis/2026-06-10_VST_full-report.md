## Executive Summary

Vistra is the largest competitive independent power producer in the US (~41 GW of nuclear, gas, solar and storage, plus the TXU retail arm), and one of the two flagship "AI datacenter power" plays alongside Constellation. The stock is in a strange place: **the business keeps beating while the stock keeps falling.** Q1 2026 delivered record results — revenue +43.4% YoY, an adjusted-EBITDA beat, reaffirmed 2026 guidance, credit-rating upgrades to investment grade, and a completed $6.13B buyback — yet the stock dropped ~8% after the print and is now **-37% from its $219.82 high, sitting ~4% above its 52-week low** ($132.66). Today it fell another 5.25% to $138.54 with no ticker-specific news found — this is a de-rating of the whole AI-power theme, not a Vistra problem.

## Fundamental Analysis

**Business quality — genuine moat.** Vistra owns the second-largest competitive nuclear fleet in the US (Comanche Peak plus the Energy Harbor plants), assets that are effectively irreplaceable — new nuclear takes a decade-plus to license and build. The integrated retail business (TXU Energy) hedges generation against power-price swings, which is why Vistra's earnings hold up better through commodity cycles than pure merchant generators. In January 2026 it announced a **$4.7B acquisition of Cogentrix** (~5,500 MW of modern gas across PJM, ISO-NE and ERCOT) aimed squarely at datacenter and industrial demand.

**Growth — accelerating.** Annual revenue: $13.7B → $14.8B → $17.2B → $17.7B, with Q1 2026 up **+43.4% YoY**. Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan raised targets after the quarter.

**Earnings quality caveat.** GAAP net income fell to $944M from $2.66B — this is mostly mark-to-market noise on power hedges, standard for IPPs, not a deterioration in the underlying business (adjusted EBITDA hit a record). It's why TTM P/E (23x) and forward P/E (12.6x) diverge so sharply: the $10.96 forward EPS reflects the real earnings power. ROE (42.9%) and P/B (17.9x) are both meaningless here — a decade of buybacks has shrunk book equity to $5.1B.

**Balance sheet — the real weak point.** $20.1B debt vs $785M cash, current ratio 0.90, and FCF has fallen three years straight ($3.78B → $2.48B → $1.32B) as growth capex ramps — and Cogentrix adds $4.7B more. The recent investment-grade upgrades soften this, but in a higher-for-longer rate world this leverage is the bear's best argument.

## Bull vs Bear

**Bull:** (1) 12.6x forward P/E and PEG 0.44 for a company growing revenue 43% with record EBITDA — cheapest the AI-power story has been since it started; (2) structural electricity demand growth from datacenters with hyperscaler PPA optionality, plus ERCOT summer scarcity pricing season starting now; (3) management is an elite capital allocator — share count down ~30% since 2021; (4) 17 analysts, STRONG_BUY, $99–320 range, $225 mean.

**Bear:** (1) the AI-power premium is deflating market-wide and nobody rings a bell at the bottom — the same stock traded at ~$60 in early 2024 before the narrative; (2) $20B debt + rising capex + falling FCF leaves little cushion if power prices soften; (3) regulatory overhang on co-location deals and ERCOT market design; (4) a break of $132 support opens air down to ~$110-120 on the chart.
